Three architectures, four stages

One agent, a decentralised swarm of three with no shared state, and a centralised swarm of three under an orchestrator, each given the same total budget and byte-identical inputs, measured at four stages of the DELPHI stack: writing a nanopublication, formalising a claim in Lean 4, specifying a design the studio accepts, and reporting what validate_generation actually said. Graded by the repository's own checks, not by a checker written for the experiment.

Every run, traceable to its log

Thirty-six cells: three arms × four stages × three tasks. Harness failures, budget cap stops and architecture failures are three different columns.

What the merge rule threw away

The decentralised arm merges by a fixed, oracle-free medoid vote: keep the candidate most agreed with by the other two. That rule never consults a grader, so it can discard a better answer. How often it did is a property of the merge rule, not of the architecture, and is reported as such.
