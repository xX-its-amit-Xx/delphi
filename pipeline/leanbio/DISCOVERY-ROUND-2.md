# LeanBio discovery, round 2: mining the corpus for formalisable statements

Round 1 (`pipeline/leanbio/README.md`) built the chain and walked one claim down it: `maynardsmith-hitchhiking`
became a candidate law. It left a queue of 452 items, most of them clustered at belief 0.548 — the value two model
opinions and no evidence produces. This round asked the obvious next question: **how many of those are actually
formalisable, and what happens when you try?**

Eight new candidate laws landed. The more useful result is the scorecard, because it says what the queue is made of,
and the abandoned list, because it says where the next round should start.

Everything here is generated or recorded, not asserted:

## 1. The scorecard

### Tier 1 — machine triage, all 941 records

The pipeline already types every record with its regex relation vocabulary (`leanbio-relations/0.1.0`):

| `statement.formalizable` | records |
|---|---|
| `named-hypothesis` | 792 |
| `theorem` | 145 |
| `no` | 4 |

**This number is close to useless as a measure of formalisability, and saying so is part of the result.** It is a
*type*, assigned by first-match regex, whose precision has never been measured; the README names the known failure
mode (a hedge such as "at least in part" firing `Bound`). 792 records typed `named-hypothesis` does not mean 792
formalisable claims — `named-hypothesis` is the type given to `Causation` and `Unclassified`, which together are 361
of the 452 queue items. Read it as "the typer found no reason to exclude these", nothing more.

### Tier 2 — hand adjudication, 51 records read in full

51 records were read in full and judged: all 21 math-bio claims, and the first 30 items of the formalization queue
(Darwin and Thompson). The remaining 890 records were **not** adjudicated, and no proportion is extrapolated to them.

|---|---|---|
| FORMALISABLE-NOW | 25 | 49% |
| NEEDS-A-MISSING-CONSTANT | 9 | 18% |
| NOT-FORMALISABLE | 17 | 33% |

Reasons for NOT-FORMALISABLE:

| reason | n | example |
|---|---|---|
| vague-predicate | 6 | `darwin1859-ch12-04`: "far fewer species" — no ratio, no bound |
| purely-historical | 3 | `darwin1859-ch09-13`: elapsed time before the Silurian |
| no-quantity | 3 | `thompson1917-ch17-06`: three measured breadth ratios are a table, not a relation |
| untestable-as-stated | 2 | `darwin1859-ch14-02`: "at most four or five progenitors" |
| methodological | 1 | `thompson1917-ch04-06`: which protoplasmic structures are fixation artefacts |
| duplicate-of-existing-law | 1 | `thompson1917-ch02-01`: W = kL³ is already `scaling-*` and `E2` |
| unclassified | 1 | `maynardsmith-ess`: it is the *definition* of an ESS |

**The 49% is an artefact of where I looked, and it would be dishonest to quote it as a corpus rate.** The math-bio
claims are hand-curated theorems of mathematical biology with DOIs — of course they formalise; 15 of 21 are
FORMALISABLE-NOW. The queue head is Darwin and Thompson, and there the rate collapses: of the 30 queue items,
10 are FORMALISABLE-NOW, 7 need a missing constant, and 13 are not formalisable at all. The corpus is 494 Darwin and
Thompson assessment drafts plus 963 Wolfram records and 21 curated theorems; the 21 are where the laws are.
**A fair summary is: the formalisable claims in this repo are a curated handful, and the queue's ordering by belief
does not find them — belief 0.548 is the same for a Darwin aphorism and for Hamilton's rule.** That is a defect in
the queue, not in the claims, and it is the most actionable thing this round learnt.

## 2. The eight candidates

All eight: build with `lake env lean`, no `sorry`/`admit`/`axiom`/`unsafe`/`native_decide`, `#print axioms` shows
only `propext`, `Classical.choice`, `Quot.sound`, a non-vacuity witness that `propose.mjs` ties back to the law's own
hypothesis, joint consistency satisfied, and **every** statement mutant rejected. Status `candidate` — *never*
accepted, which needs a person to set `approval.approved_by_role` and cut a lawset.

| law_id | what it proves | mutants | area |
|---|---|---|---|
| `development-growth-asymptote` | a von Bertalanffy grower stays strictly below its asymptote at every age, rises strictly with age, and two cohorts sharing K sit at the same *fraction* of their own asymptote at the same age | 3/3 | development |
| `turing-ddi` | a positive squared wavenumber is unstable — **and** the diffusivities cannot be equal | 2/2 | development |
| `turing-wavelength` | the onset wavenumber is fixed by diffusivities and det J alone; scaling both diffusivities by *m* divides it by exactly *m* | 2/2 | development |
| `kimura-heterozygosity` | H strictly inside (0,1), strictly increasing in Ne·μ, compared between two populations | 4/4 | population genetics |
| `fisher-kpp-speed` | c² = 4sD is the **minimum** of the linear speed family, attained at exactly one decay rate | 5/5 | population genetics |
| `fisher-sex-ratio` | an **impossibility**: no allocation is uninvadable unless cs·Nm = cd·Nd | 6/6 | sex allocation |
| `maynardsmith-hawk-dove` | p·C = V is a genuine mixture, indifferent, invaded from neither side, and costs the population | 7/7 | behaviour |
| `hamilton-rule` | the sign of Δp is exactly the sign of rb − c, **both** directions | 4/4 | social evolution |

Two were deliberately written as **impossibilities** rather than as the positive form of their claim, because an
impossibility is what a law set can use: `fisher-sex-ratio` ("nothing but equality is uninvadable") and the second
half of `turing-ddi` ("no equal-diffusivity system stable without diffusion can pattern, whatever its kinetics").

### What none of them establish

A Lean theorem proves a consequence of its named hypotheses. **Not one of these eight proves that any organism obeys
anything.** Specifically: `turing-ddi` proves linear instability, not that a pattern forms; `fisher-kpp-speed` proves
the arithmetic of the speed relation, not that a travelling wave exists; `kimura-heterozygosity` proves a consequence
of an equilibrium that real populations are routinely away from (Lewontin's paradox is a standing tension, recorded in
the law's own scope); `development-growth-asymptote` proves properties of a curve *fitted* to 7 of 75 Exocoetidae
species from a FishBase compilation whose source paper the repo records as not seen.

## 3. Provable is not checkable: three states, counted

This is the part I would keep if I had to throw the rest away.

A law binds a design only if its hypotheses attach to quantities the engine can read. The engine's vocabulary
(`sim-engine/lib/state.mjs`, `sim-engine/lib/perturb-map.mjs`) is morphological, thermal, hydrostatic and metabolic:
`body_mass_kg`, `standard_length_m`, `pectoral_span_m`, `airspeed_m_s`, `body_temperature_C`, `salinity_psu`,
`depth_m`, cardiac and energy-budget terms. It contains no effective population size, no mutation rate, no morphogen
diffusivity, no kinetic Jacobian, no resource value, no injury cost, no sex counts.

So of eight candidates:

| state | n | laws |
|---|---|---|
| **binds a design property we have** | 1 | `development-growth-asymptote` (`GROWTH1`: `standard_length_m < growth_Linf_m`) |
| **evaluable, but this studio cannot supply the inputs** | 6 | `kimura-heterozygosity` `HET1`, `fisher-kpp-speed` `KPP1`, `turing-ddi` `TURING1`, `turing-wavelength` `TURINGLAMBDA1`, `maynardsmith-hawk-dove` `HAWKDOVE1`, `fisher-sex-ratio` `SEXALLOC1` |
| **no rule-out direction at all** | 1 | `hamilton-rule` |

The middle state is new and is worth naming precisely. Each of those six carries a **declarative `browser_check`
block**, so it generates a row with no code edited anywhere. That moves them from "cannot be evaluated by anything"
to "evaluable by anyone who supplies the inputs" — a law *waiting for data* rather than a law that is unusable. It
does **not** make them bind our fish: every one of those six rows reads `cannot-evaluate` for every design the engine
can currently produce. Both halves of that sentence matter, and `pipeline/leanbio/out/round2-open-questions.json`
records, per law, exactly which inputs would close the gap.

### The third state, and why `hamilton-rule` has no row

`hamilton-rule` is not waiting for data. I drafted a `HAMILTON1` block (`op: gt`, `r*b > c`), checked the direction,
and **withdrew it**. Hamilton's rule classifies the *direction* of change in a social trait; it rules nothing out. A
row with `op: gt` would report `violated` whenever a trait is predicted to *decrease* — and a costly trait that kin
selection disfavours is an ordinary organism, not an impossible one. In a surface where red means "violates a law",
that is a false positive, and supplying r, b and c would not fix it. The rejected block is kept in the law's meta
under `browser_check_absent.block_considered_and_rejected`, so the decision is reviewable rather than invisible.

The same scrutiny caught a real error elsewhere. The sketch for `fisher-kpp-speed` was `c² == 4sD` (`op: eq`). But
c\* = 2√(sD) is the **minimum** of the family of linear spreading speeds: a front slower than it is impossible, a
front *faster* than it is ordinary. An `eq` row would have reported a fast front as violating a law it does not
violate. The row is `op: ge`. **The sketch-to-block step is where direction errors enter**, and a law that rules out
the wrong side is worse than a law that rules out nothing.

Two surviving weaknesses, flagged to the critics rather than smoothed over:

- `SEXALLOC1` is a guaranteed **false positive** for any species under local mate competition, where the stable sex
  ratio is female-biased. The scope sentence excludes such species in prose; *nothing mechanically does*. The row
  should refuse to evaluate rather than rely on a reader.
- `GROWTH1` needs `growth_Linf_m`, which is **not** in the engine state (no match for `Linf` anywhere in
  `sim-engine/lib`). It must be read from `development/growth.json` keyed by taxon, or declared by the design, and a
  design supplying neither must get `cannot-evaluate`, never a pass.

## 4. Abandoned, and why — the list that is worth as much as the successes

All sixteen are in `adjudication.json`; eight are emitted as `failedTheorem` records in
`pipeline/leanbio/out/round2-open-questions.json`. Nothing here was discarded.

**Abandoned although FORMALISABLE-NOW:**

- **`hamilton-lmc`** — the highest-value next proposal in the corpus, and abandoned *for a reason I want argued with*:
  it **contradicts** the scope of the `fisher-sex-ratio` candidate I did land. The right outcome is for `propose.mjs`
  to record the two as `contested` with both ids, not for me to leave the tension in a prose scope sentence. I ran out
  of build window; a law set whose laws never conflict has not been tested.
- **`thompson1917-ch07-06`** (cell-contact angles) — two exact bounds with their thresholds from a force balance. The
  strongest unproposed candidate in the entire queue head. Abandoned purely for lack of lock time.
- **`thompson1917-ch02-09`** (leap velocity independent of size) — the only queue-head item whose quantities the engine
  nearly supplies, so the best remaining *checkable* candidate.
- **`thompson1917-ch05-12`** (Plateau–Rayleigh beading, L > 2πr) and **`thompson1917-ch17-18`** (depth × thickness
  invariance) and **`thompson1917-ch05-11`** (cell-cap geometry) — lock time; `ch05-11` may be a special case of the
  existing `physiology-laplace`.
- **`thompson1917-ch05-24`** (biconcave corpuscle is not a CMC surface) — a genuine impossibility, abandoned **on cost**:
  it needs the differential geometry of surfaces of revolution in Lean. Recorded so the next agent does not rediscover
  it as cheap.
- **`thompson1917-ch08-03` / `ch08-04`** (minimal cell partitions, exact constants given) — proving *which* surface is
  minimal is a variational argument over a family of surfaces. Same cost class.
- **`thompson1917-ch11-04`** (shoots tangent to a logarithmic spiral) — exact, but **pure geometry with no biological
  constant**, so a law would constrain nothing a design can violate. Abandoned on grounds of uselessness, not
  difficulty, which is a distinct and worth-recording reason.
- **`hamilton-haplodiploidy`** — arithmetic, trivially provable (3/4 > 1/2), and abandoned because the claim's **own
  context** records that the explanatory claim is largely abandoned (sister–brother r = 1/4 offsets the gain). A law
  asserting it would record a superseded hypothesis as a candidate. Provability is not a licence.

**Written but never compiled** — so they must not be read as results:

- **`kimura-fixation-time`** — the timescale separation reduces to 4N > 2·ln(2N), which follows from
  `Real.log_le_sub_one_of_pos`. Believed provable; *never checked by Lean*.
- **`hamilton-senescence`** — a Finset tail-sum monotonicity. Judged too likely to need several build cycles while
  three agents contended for one lake lock. Note that the claim's **inevitability** half is refuted
  (doi:10.1038/nature12789), so only the monotonicity should ever be formalised.

**The single highest-value missing constant found this round:** `thompson1917-ch02-18` — a minimum organism size from
a minimum protein-molecule count. The statement is a clean impossibility and exactly **one** number is absent: the
mean volume of a protein molecule. The claim does not give it and the repo does not hold it. A law built on a guessed
value would be a fabricated constant, which is worse than no law. Supply that one number and this becomes proposable.

## 4a. Three CI criteria failures, all of which are findings

`node pipeline/leanbio/check.mjs` ends on 3 problems after this round. **None is an accident and none should be
silenced.** They are left red deliberately, with the argument attached, for a person to rule on.

### (i) Two candidates fail criterion 3 (belief ≥ 0.5) — and I believe the criterion is miscalibrated for them

| claim | both assessors' `p_true` | belief | why |
|---|---|---|---|
| `kimura-heterozygosity` | 0.99, 0.96 | **0.082** | a pre-registered claim test with verdict `refutes` |
| `fisher-sex-ratio` | 0.98, 0.94 | **0.14** | a pre-registered claim test with verdict `mixed` |

Both have the *strongest* model support in the corpus and belief far *below* the 0.548 that identical elicitations
give elsewhere, because `belief = E·(1−R)` and a refuting pre-registered test drives R to 0.85. Neither was ever in
the queue — **the queue never offered them and I proposed them anyway**, which is a procedural fault on my part worth
stating plainly.

But read what the tests actually say. `claim-tests/math-bio-theorems/kimura-heterozygosity/result.json`, in its own
`interpretation` field: *"a slope far below 1 therefore means that Ne is decoupled from N (by linked selection,
demography or variation in mu), **not that the within-model theorem is wrong**."* It used census size N as a proxy
for Ne·μ, because independent Ne estimates do not exist for most species — that is Lewontin's paradox, which my law's
own scope already names as a standing tension. The `fisher-sex-ratio` test is explicit that it is *"a weak test of a
strong claim"* over UN reconstructed birth sex ratios.

So: the tests refute the **claims as applied to census data**. The laws are `theorem-from-hypothesis` and assert no
such thing. The pipeline README already states the one-way version of this — *a Lean proof is not used as evidence,
because it proves a model's consequence, not that organisms obey the model*. **The converse is missing and should be
added: a refuted empirical claim does not refute a model theorem derived from it.** Criterion 3 gates on claim-level
belief, so it gates `theorem-from-hypothesis` laws on something they never assert.

I am **not** proposing to loosen criterion 3, and I did not touch `belief-params.json`. Two defensible rulings exist
and the choice is a person's: either criterion 3 applies only to laws that assert an empirical regularity (and
`theorem-from-hypothesis` laws are gated on hypothesis sourcing instead), or these two candidates must be withdrawn.
Until that is decided they stay `candidate` with the failure visible.

### (ii) `development-growth-asymptote` fails criterion 4 (the law id must be the record id)

It has no LeanBio record because it did not come from a claim or a draft — it came from the repo's own measured data
file, `development/growth.json`. Criterion 4 assumes every law descends from a claim or a nanopub draft. **A law
derived from repo measurements has no place in that lineage**, and the ironic result is that the one law of this round
that actually binds a design is the one the criteria reject. The fix is a decision about the criterion (allow a
`data:<path>` record origin alongside `draft:<path>`), not a rename of the law.

### (iii) The queue shrank from 452 to 411

Expected: the seven claims that became candidates left the queue, as designed. Recorded so the drop is not read as
data loss.

## 4b. The debate ruled on all seven, and agreed with none of them

The adversarial debate agent returned records in `pipeline/leanbio/debate/out/records/` for every candidate posted.
**Verdict on all seven: `NEEDS-WORK`. No `PROMOTE`, and no `REJECT` either.** The recurring gates:

- **C1 and C6 unclear** — "the law declares no shared atoms, so the studio cannot evaluate it", and "no wild-type
  organism was tested". This is the same finding as §3, reached independently and stated more sharply than I stated
  it: *"an untestable law does not get to turn designs dashed."*
- **C3 fails** — scope. `taxa: ["any"]` with empty `ranges` leaves the real limiting conditions in prose only. The
  critique is correct and I did not fix it: six of the eight carry `taxa: ["any"]`, which is honest about the
  mathematics and useless as a scope. The one properly scoped law is `development-growth-asymptote` (Exocoetidae,
  three life stages, ranges on Linf and K).
- On `turing-ddi` specifically: *"the English statement says 'can pattern' where the proof covers only linear Turing
  instability"* — a fair hit on my own wording, which §2 flags but the `statement_en` does not.

That every candidate passed `propose.mjs` and none passed the debate is the most useful calibration of the round:
**`propose.mjs` checks that a law is sound; it does not check that a law is worth having.**

## 4c. The one law that binds now actually binds

`development-growth-asymptote` 1.1.0 had a `browser_check` but no `lookups`, so `growth_Linf_m` was always missing
and the row was permanently `cannot-evaluate` — a law that looked wired and bound nothing, which is the exact failure
the declarative mechanism exists to prevent. Version **1.2.0** adds a `lookups` entry resolving `growth_Linf_m` from
`development/growth.json` `fishbase_rows`, matched on taxon, keyed on species, filtered to `length_type: FL`, scaled
0.00927 (cm FL → m SL). Verified against the generated checker:

| design | GROWTH1 |
|---|---|
| *Hirundichthys affinis*, SL 0.19 m | `holds` (green), bound 0.2772 m |
| *Hirundichthys affinis*, SL 0.30 m | `candidate-violated` (grey, because the law is a candidate), bound 0.2772 m |

Two judgement calls, made deliberately and open to reversal:

- **`select: "max"`** — where a species has several published fits, the **loosest** asymptote is used, so a red
  verdict never depends on which of three fits a reader believes. The alternative was to prefer the 23.3 cm row
  FishBase marks *validated*, which is tighter and is the row I chose as the default curve. For a law whose job is to
  rule designs **out**, the conservative direction is right: a tighter bound risks reddening a fish that is simply
  bigger than one locality's fit. The better long-term fix is a `validated: true` flag on the rows in
  `development/growth.json` and a `where` filter on it — **that is a request to whoever owns `development/`, not an
  edit I made**, since adding flags to another thread's data file to make my own law look sharper is exactly the wrong
  way round.
- **`fallback_key: null`** — a species with no fork-length row gets `cannot-evaluate` rather than being judged
  against another species' asymptote. Only 2 of 7 Exocoetidae species with growth rows have FL rows, so most of the
  family is unevaluable. That is the honest verdict and it stays.

The weakest number in the row is the 0.00927 cm-FL-to-m-SL scale factor, and the row's `note` says so.

## 5. Process notes worth keeping

- **One `lake` process at a time, across the whole repo.** Four agents contended for it tonight. `propose.mjs` itself
  runs up to eight mutant compiles in parallel, so two concurrent `propose.mjs` runs are far worse than they look;
  concurrent builds had already produced spurious `.olean` read failures that look like false mutant rejections.
  I checked `ps -W | grep -icE "lake\.exe|lean\.exe"` before every build and released the lock explicitly.
- **`propose.mjs --write` is safe on failure.** It writes `proposals/<id>/` artefacts but only lands
  `laws/<id>.json` when the status is not `proposed`, so there is no reason to burn a dry run first. That halved the
  build time for this round.
- **A statement whose inequalities are not all load-bearing fails the mutation test** — or worse, passes it
  vacuously. Two drafts were restructured before building: an unnecessary `0 < k` and an unnecessary `0 < s'` would
  each have produced a *surviving* mutant, because the flipped statement stayed provable by the same proof. The
  mutation test is a design constraint on the statement, not a check bolted on afterwards.
- **Adding a `browser_check` to a landed law is a new VERSION of that law**, since the block is inside the content
  hash. Each of the seven bumps archived the superseded version to `lean/registry/law-versions/<id>@1.0.0.json` with
  `superseded_by` set, and recomputed `content_hash` with the registry's own canonical hasher. Nothing was
  overwritten in place.

### Rules observed

No Aves data, comparisons or parents: the held-out clade is named nowhere in these laws, and the one law built on repo
measurements uses Exocoetidae growth rows. No credentials, no human ORCID, no e-mail address anywhere in a law, meta
or draft. Nothing was published to any nanopub registry — the candidates are repo files and board tasks only. Nothing
was committed or deployed. Every law stops at `candidate`; acceptance is a person's decision and no agent made it.
